\begin{abstract}
随着云计算、移动互联网、人工智能和物联网技术的快速发展，端到端网络传输正在从单一固定终端和局域高质量链路，扩展到跨地域远距离传输、蜂窝/无线/卫星等多接入网络，以及手机、笔记本、台式机和边缘设备等多模态终端并存的复杂场景。传统基于丢包的拥塞控制算法（如TCP NewReno、CUBIC）存在拥塞信号滞后、高带宽延迟积（Bandwidth-Delay Product，BDP）网络收敛缓慢、无法区分拥塞程度等固有缺陷；而基于延迟的算法（如TCP Vegas、BBR）则面临往返时延（Round-Trip Time，RTT）测量不稳定、与丢包算法共存时公平性差等问题。本文提出Swift算法——一种基于强化学习辅助的多信号融合自适应网络拥塞控制方法。该算法名称寓意"雨燕"，象征着高吞吐量（High Throughput）与低延迟（Low Latency）的设计目标，如同雨燕般轻盈敏捷、反应迅速。

Swift算法的核心创新收敛为三点：（1）面向远距离与异构终端传输的11维有效RL观测子空间设计，该子空间嵌入于15元素OpenGym \texttt{Box}传输容器，另含4项跨进程路由用的元数据通道，将显式拥塞通知（Explicit Congestion Notification，ECN）状态、拥塞控制状态机（Congestion Algorithm State）状态、拥塞事件类型、RTT和在途字节等协议栈信息纳入强化学习状态表示；（2）多信号融合拥塞判定与差异化响应机制，采用分层优先级机制综合分析丢包、ECN、超时和拥塞状态信号，并针对不同拥塞类型采用不同的窗口保留因子（Window Retention Factor）——丢包0.70、ECN标记0.75、超时0.50，同时引入连续缩减保护机制（最多连续3次缩减后保持窗口）和降窗后4个ACK事件冻结机制，以区分拥塞信号的性质和严重程度并抑制窗口快速反弹；（3）强化学习辅助的自适应融合控制策略，根据RTT膨胀比、奖励信号的指数移动平均（Exponential Moving Average，EMA）、连续增长趋势等多因素动态调整乘性增加因子$\alpha$（取值范围$[0.85, 1.30]$，基准值1.10），并采用窗口化最大值过滤（Windowed Max-filter）估计BDP，在拥塞避免阶段围绕$\alpha \times BDP$目标窗口进行有界步长的渐进拉升或回落。

本文基于ns-3网络仿真平台和ns3-gym/OpenGym强化学习框架实现了Swift算法，采用ZeroMQ进程间通信实现仿真进程与决策进程的同步协作。在覆盖近端高速、无线接入、5G移动网络、城域广域网（Wide Area Network，WAN）和卫星通信等21种场景、84组实验中，经数据质量审计筛选出9种具备全部四种协议完整对比数据的主要场景；为进一步评估算法在多模态终端和共享链路中面对突发性非响应式跨流量时的鲁棒性，仿真器在哑铃拓扑上额外引入\texttt{enable\_udp\_burst}控制开关，构建了"纯TCP"与"TCP + 800\,Mbps UDP Burst"的A/B严格对照实验矩阵。\textbf{稳态实验结果}表明：在近端高速场景（10G/25G/20G），Swift保持与NewReno/CUBIC相当的吞吐量（差距$<$0.01\%），同时将排队延迟降低83\%$\sim$93\%（从1.9$\sim$2.3ms降至0.14$\sim$0.36ms）；在中速网络场景（5G eMBB、城域WAN、WiFi AC/AX），延迟降低9\%$\sim$24\%且全部实现零丢包；在GEO卫星长距离场景中，凭借零丢包特性实现吞吐量相对NewReno/CUBIC均值提升28.8\%。\textbf{跨流量鲁棒性实验}进一步表明：在全部17个完整对照场景中，Swift是\emph{唯一}维持零丢包率的算法（CUBIC最大新增丢包率$+$3.45\%、BBR $+$1.58\%）；Swift的吞吐量回落幅度最小（均值仅19.78\%，BBR为崩塌式55.55\%）；且近端高速场景的延迟降幅被完整保留（S1$\sim$S3 仍为$-$82.28\%/$-$92.91\%/$-$91.02\%），GEO卫星场景中Swift的吞吐量优势由 $+$28.8\% 进一步放大至 $+$86.8\%。本文同时如实披露了Swift在中低速和中等RTT场景中存在小幅度的延迟抬升，以及WAN场景吞吐量仍有进一步上探空间，其机理在于$\alpha \times BDP$窗口目标机制在高RTT下的扩张幅度仍可进一步收紧，相应的算法层改进方向已在第5章给出。

\keywords{拥塞控制，强化学习，多信号融合，显式拥塞通知，自适应参数调优，远距离传输，异构终端}

\end{abstract}




\begin{englishabstract}

With the rapid development of cloud computing, mobile Internet, artificial intelligence, and Internet-of-Things technologies, end-to-end network transmission is expanding from fixed terminals and local high-quality links to long-distance cross-region paths, cellular/wireless/satellite access networks, and heterogeneous endpoint environments involving phones, laptops, desktops, edge devices, and other terminal classes. Traditional loss-based congestion control algorithms (such as TCP NewReno and CUBIC) suffer from delayed congestion signals, slow convergence in high Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP) networks, and inability to distinguish congestion severity. Meanwhile, delay-based algorithms (such as TCP Vegas and BBR) face unstable Round-Trip Time (RTT) measurements and poor fairness when coexisting with loss-based algorithms.

This paper proposes Swift, a reinforcement-learning-assisted multi-signal fusion adaptive network congestion control algorithm. The name ``Swift'' symbolizes the design goals of High Throughput and Low Latency, like a swift that is light, agile, and responsive. The core innovations of Swift are consolidated into three aspects: (1) an 11-dimensional effective RL observation subspace for long-distance and heterogeneous endpoint transmission, embedded in a 15-element OpenGym \texttt{Box} transport container with four additional metadata channels for inter-process routing, which incorporates Explicit Congestion Notification (ECN) status, Congestion Algorithm (CA) state, congestion event types, RTT, and bytes in flight into the reinforcement learning state representation; (2) a multi-signal congestion determination and differentiated response mechanism, which hierarchically analyzes packet loss, ECN, timeout, and congestion-state signals and applies different window retention factors for different congestion types (packet loss: 0.70, ECN: 0.75, timeout: 0.50), together with consecutive decrease protection and a four-ACK post-decrease freeze mechanism; and (3) a reinforcement-learning-assisted adaptive fusion control strategy, which dynamically adjusts the multiplicative increase factor $\alpha \in [0.85, 1.30]$ based on RTT inflation gradients, reward EMA, and consecutive growth trends, estimates BDP through a windowed max-filter, and moves the congestion window toward the $\alpha \times BDP$ target with bounded pull-up or drain steps during congestion avoidance.

We implemented Swift based on the ns-3 network simulation platform and ns3-gym/OpenGym reinforcement learning framework, with ZeroMQ inter-process communication for synchronous interaction between the simulation process and the decision process. Extensive experiments across 21 scenarios covering near-end high-speed links, wireless access, 5G, metropolitan/WAN, and satellite communications (84 experiment groups) yielded 9 primary scenarios with complete four-protocol comparison data after data quality auditing; to further evaluate robustness against bursty non-responsive cross-traffic in heterogeneous endpoint and shared-link environments, the simulator additionally exposes an \texttt{enable\_udp\_burst} switch that constructs a strict A/B experimental matrix between ``pure TCP'' and ``TCP + 800\,Mbps UDP Burst'' settings on the same dumbbell topology. \textbf{Steady-state results} demonstrate that: in near-end high-speed scenarios (10G/25G/20G), Swift maintains throughput comparable to NewReno/CUBIC (gap $<$ 0.01\%) while reducing queuing latency by 83\%--93\% (from 1.9--2.3\,ms to 0.14--0.36\,ms); in medium-speed scenarios (5G eMBB, metro WAN, WiFi AC/AX), latency is reduced by 9\%--24\% with zero packet loss; in GEO satellite long-distance scenarios, zero-loss behavior yields a 28.8\% throughput improvement over the NewReno/CUBIC mean. \textbf{Cross-traffic robustness experiments} further show that across all 17 complete comparison scenarios, Swift is the \emph{only} algorithm to maintain zero packet loss (CUBIC adds up to $+$3.45\% loss, BBR up to $+$1.58\%); Swift's throughput degradation is the smallest (mean 19.78\%, compared with BBR's collapse of 55.55\%); and the near-end high-speed latency advantage is fully preserved ($-$82.28\%/$-$92.91\%/$-$91.02\% for S1--S3), while Swift's GEO satellite throughput advantage expands from $+$28.8\% to $+$86.8\%. Experiments also faithfully report that Swift exhibits a mild latency uplift in medium-to-low-speed and medium-RTT scenarios, and that there remains room for further throughput exploration in WAN, attributable to the expansion magnitude of the $\alpha \times BDP$ window target under high RTT, with corresponding algorithm-level refinement directions presented in Chapter 5.

\end{englishabstract}
